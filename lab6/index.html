1. Dataset and hierarchy

Source table — lab6_assignment_gdp.csv
27 countries, one row each, with continent, area, country, gdp_billion_usd and gdp_status (Increase 15, Unchanged 7, Decrease 5). Synthetic, supplied for visualization practice.
Hierarchy — lab6_assignment_gdp.json
World → 6 continents → 15 areas → 27 countries, built by convert_gdp.py, which groups the flat rows one level at a time with pandas and recurses. Each leaf keeps both attributes, gdp and status; continent and area totals are not stored but summed in the browser with d3.hierarchy().sum().

3. Design description

GDP amount is rectangle area, the channel a treemap is built on, so part-to-whole reads directly: the United States and China together fill over half of each picture. GDP status is colour on a diverging scheme, blue for Increase and red for Decrease, with neutral grey for Unchanged, since “no change” should look like the absence of a signal. Status is also printed as ▲/●/▼ inside each labelled cell so it never relies on hue alone. The hierarchy is containment: two tones of framed header strip, continent and area, so structure never competes with status for colour.

Squarify keeps rectangles close to square, which makes areas easy to compare and leaves room for labels; the cost is that position means nothing, and a continent's areas land wherever they fit. Slice-and-dice alternates direction by depth — continents are columns, areas are rows, countries are columns again — so the hierarchy is readable at a glance and order is stable, but small members become slivers (Africa is 14 pixels wide). Zoom and tooltips recover what it squeezes out.

4. What the two views show

Growth is concentrated rather than spread: 15 of 27 countries increased, but they carry 72% of all GDP ($61,290B) because the United States, China and India are among them. The five decreases are mid-to-large economies — Japan, Germany, Italy, Brazil and Nigeria — so in treemap A the red cells are few but not small. In treemap B the same fact is easier to locate: Europe's column is the only one with two red cells, one in each of two different areas.
