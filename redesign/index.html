Redesigning the World Happiness Rankings

Every March, the headline “Finland is the happiest country in the world” is written from one chart: Figure 2.1 of the World Happiness Report 2026. It ranks 147 countries by how their residents rate their own lives, then splits each score into the parts that six factors (income, social support, health, freedom, generosity and corruption) explain. Below are the original chart and an interactive D3 redesign built from the report’s own published data.

The critique of the original and the reasoning behind each design decision are in the separate critique and redesign report (PDF).

Data shown
147 countries and territories. For each one: the average answer to the Cantril ladder question (“imagine a ladder from 0, the worst possible life for you, to 10, the best possible life”) in the Gallup World Poll, pooled over 2023–2025 at roughly 1,000 respondents per country per year; a 95% confidence interval for that average; and a 95% range for the country’s rank. Each score is then split into seven stacked sub-bars: the part a regression attributes to each of six factors (log GDP per capita, social support, healthy life expectancy, freedom to make life choices, generosity, perceptions of corruption), measured from Dystopia, an imaginary country with the world’s lowest value of every factor and a ladder score of 1.16, plus a final “Dystopia + residual” sub-bar for whatever is left.

The original, as printed across three pages of the report (screenshots rendered from the PDF by build_data.py). Click a page to open it at full size.

2. The redesign

Four linked views replace the three pages of stacked bars. The overview shows where every country stands. A waterfall explains one country’s score step by step, and a second country can be placed beside it. Strip plots compare every country on each factor, and a ranked list is used for looking countries up. Click a country in any view to make it country A. Every value comes from redesign_whr2026.csv, which build_data.py builds from the report’s own spreadsheet.
