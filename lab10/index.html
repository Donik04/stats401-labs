The two maps of Lab 9, a choropleth and a GDP cartogram of the 50 largest economies of 2025, extended with three new inputs: voice commands, hand gestures seen by the webcam, and webcam eye gaze. The maps are not rebuilt. This page loads Lab 9's own lab9.js, and lab10.js only adds inputs. Every input ends in the same few map actions that the mouse, the buttons and the keyboard also reach, so each novel input has a conventional fallback.

1. Which task each input serves

Input Visualization task Why this input fits it
Voice Locate an economy by name; filter to a continent Singapore and Hong Kong are a few pixels on the land map; with a mouse they have to be found and zoomed to before they can be clicked. Saying the name skips both. A continent is also a name rather than a place to point at.
Hand gesture Step through the ranking; zoom in and out “Next economy” is a coarse command repeated many times, which suits a swipe. It works away from the keyboard, for example when presenting the maps on a projector.
Eye gaze Indicate the country being looked at The eyes are already on the target. Webcam gaze is noisy, so it only indicates after a dwell; voice or Space confirms. It works best on the cartogram, where economies are large.

2. How to use

Nothing listens or watches until you press its button. The browser then asks for permission, a red indicator stays in the bottom-left corner while a device is on, and Stop releases it. Camera frames are processed inside this page (MediaPipe and WebGazer) and are never stored or uploaded. Speech is different: Chrome and Edge send the audio to their speech service to transcribe it. Voice needs Chrome, Edge or Safari; Firefox has no speech recognition. The camera needs https or http://localhost.

Eye gaze

  1. Press Start eye gaze and allow the camera.
  2. Calibrate: click each of the nine dots five times while looking at it. Keep your head still afterwards.
  3. Look at a country for 0.8 s: the ring around the gaze point fills and the country is highlighted.
  4. Say “select” or press Space to select it.

Fallback. Everything above also works without a microphone or camera: the mouse works as in Lab 9 (hover, click, drag, scroll to zoom), the bar above the maps has buttons for every action, the keyboard has ← → previous / next, Z zoom to the selection, Esc reset and V to start listening, and voice commands can be typed into the box below.

6. Design reflection

I extended the Lab 9 GDP choropleth and cartogram with voice, hand-gesture and eye-gaze input. Voice serves locating and filtering: “Show Asia” scopes both maps to a continent, and “Highlight Singapore” finds a country that is a few pixels wide on the land map, which the mouse reaches only after zooming. Gestures serve sequential browsing: a swipe steps to the next or previous economy by rank, so the ranking can be walked through like slides, and only six gestures keep recognition reliable. Gaze only indicates: a 0.8-second dwell highlights a country and “select” or Space confirms, which avoids the Midas touch problem. The limits are recognition errors. Short names are easily misheard (“Chile” as “chilly”), so the parser tries the recogniser's alternative transcripts and refuses near misses that fit two countries. An open palm at the start of a swipe can look like “reset”, so a pose must be held still before it fires. Webcam gaze is off by roughly 100–200 pixels and drifts when the head moves, so it works on the large cartogram shapes but not on small European countries on the choropleth.

Implementation notes

One action layer. Each input follows the lab's pipeline (input → recognition → interpretation → command → D3 update → feedback), and they meet at the command object. commands.js parses a transcript into a command such as {type: "region", region: "Asia"}; a gesture label maps to a fixed command; every command runs through one function in lab10.js that changes Lab 9's shared state and reports what it did to the panel, the action log and the toast. Lab 9's file gained only one handle object, so zooms can be started from outside it; its own page is unchanged.

Voice uses the Web Speech API (en-US, one utterance per recognition, interim results shown while you speak, three alternatives). The first alternative that parses wins. Country names come from the dataset plus common short forms, and a near miss of one or two letters is accepted only when it fits exactly one country.

Gestures use MediaPipe's Gesture Recognizer in the browser (one hand, GPU when available). The six poses come from its built-in classifier; swipes are added from the 21 hand landmarks: the palm centre must move across 22% of the frame within 0.6 s, at least twice as far horizontally as vertically. The camera image is not mirrored, so movement to the user's right is leftward in the image. A pose fires after 0.45 s held still and then latches until the hand changes, and a 1-second cooldown separates any two gesture commands.

Gaze uses WebGazer.js with ridge regression and its Kalman filter, trained by the calibration clicks; calibration data is not saved between visits. The page averages the last six estimates, shows them as the gaze ring, and hit-tests nine points in a 24-pixel radius around it; the economy that gets at least three of the nine hits is the candidate. Countries without GDP data are ignored.

Regions come from Natural Earth's continent field, written to lab10_regions.csv by build_regions.py, so Russia counts as Europe and Turkey as Asia. Each continent has a longitude–latitude view box; the choropleth zooms to the land inside it, and the cartogram to where those same vertices ended up after the GDP distortion.
