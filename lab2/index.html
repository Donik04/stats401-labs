Assignment — Four Dimensions of Twelve Cities

Twelve cities, each carrying four variables of a different measurement type. Rather than a scatterplot, the cities are split into one small panel per region, so every city keeps its own labelled row. Hover any bar for the exact values.

Design Justification

I mapped population (ratio) to bar length. Length is the channel people compare most accurately, and because it starts at a true zero it supports the ratio statements population allows — Ivydale really is about six times Dover. Region (nominal) became the panels rather than a colour, since nominal categories have no order and separating them spatially avoids spending a colour scale on labels that carry no magnitude. That also keeps region comparisons honest: within a panel the reader compares cities, and across panels they compare regions. Temperature (interval) is the single-hue blue fill, light to dark. One hue with increasing darkness reads as ordered magnitude without implying a meaningful zero, which suits interval data. Development level (ordinal) is the row of one to three pips beside each city, because a small repeated count reads as ranked steps — Low, Medium, High — without pretending the gaps between levels are equal sizes.

What I hope a viewer notices is the cool, larger, more developed north and west against the warm, small, less developed south. The inverse relationship between temperature and population becomes visible as the darkest bars sitting consistently among the shortest.
